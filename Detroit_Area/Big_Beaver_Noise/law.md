## Troy Code of Ordinances, Chapter 88 (Nuisances)

Source: [Troy City Code, Chapter 88](https://troymi.gov/Departments/City%20Attorney/Code%20and%20Charter/Code%20Table%20of%20Contents/CH088.pdf). Text extracted from the downloaded PDF during research.

Caveat: the cached copy has 2014-era metadata and "Rev." dates. Confirm the current text before citing it in a formal complaint.

- **Sec. 9.1, Public nuisances defined:** "Whatever annoys, injures, or endangers the safety, health, comfort or repose of the public … is hereby declared to be a public nuisance."
- **Sec. 9.5, Noises:** lists unlawful noises. The list is non-exclusive ("this enumeration shall not be deemed to be exclusive").
- **Sec. 9.5(b)-(c):** noise that will "annoy or disturb the quiet, comfort, or repose of persons … in any dwelling … or of any persons in the vicinity." Closest match: a neighbor-impact test, with no decibel threshold.
- **Sec. 9.5(h), Construction noises:** applies to construction work, not roof equipment. Not cited for this problem.

### Not found

- No decibel limit for rooftop or HVAC equipment in Chapter 88.
- Troy Zoning Ordinance noise or mechanical-equipment standards were not checked. Likely place for a numeric limit or screening rule.

### Reasonable-person standard

- Chapter 88 uses a reasonable-neighbor standard ("comfort or repose," "vicinity") rather than a fixed decibel limit.
- Michigan common-law private nuisance uses the same idea: whether the interference is substantial and unreasonable to a person of ordinary sensibilities (Restatement (Second) of Torts §821F). Stated from general knowledge; not verified in this research.

## Michigan: noise and light

- **Noise is a private nuisance.** Adkins v. Thomas Solvent Co. (Mich. 1992) requires significant, unreasonable interference, and mere annoyance isn't enough. Source: [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/michigan/supreme-court/1992/88897-5.html).
- **Ordinance violation is not required.** A 2009 Michigan Court of Appeals decision let a noise-based nuisance claim proceed without an ordinance violation. Source: [michbar.org](http://www.michbar.org/opinions/appeals/2009/041609/42435.pdf). Confirm the case name and citation.
- **Private nuisance can be enjoined in circuit court.** Source: [Michigan Bar Environmental Law deskbook, ch. 13](https://connect.michbar.org/envlaw/reports/deskbook/chapter13).
- **Trespass is different.** Most U.S. courts treat noise as nuisance, not trespass. California's high court held noise alone isn't trespass. A Michigan trial-level instruction in Adams v. Cleveland-Cliffs described noise as an intangible trespass, but that doesn't settle appellate law.
- **Light is mostly local.** No statewide Michigan light-trespass statute was found. Local ordinances set limits. Ann Arbor: 0.1 foot-candles at residential lot lines. An unpublished April 2024 Michigan Court of Appeals decision (Traverse City) involved a nuisance-abatement claim under that city's lighting ordinance. No link to the opinion was confirmed; find it on the Michigan Court of Appeals site before citing.
- **Pending 2026 case:** residents near a data center in Dowagiac sued over noise under private nuisance. Pleading stage, not a ruling.
