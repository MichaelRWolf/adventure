# Photo Descriptions: Access Road Erosion, The Cut, South of Roscommon

Attachment to a letter from Michael Wolf to Jason Lewicki, Michigan DNR.

## About This Document

Michael Wolf took the five photos on a site visit. The descriptions were written with the help of Claude, an AI assistant made by Anthropic. Claude looked at each photo directly and wrote a plain-language description of what it shows and why it matters for the erosion. Michael then reviewed the descriptions against his own knowledge of the site.

Claude also read the GPS coordinates stored inside each photo file. All five photos were taken at the same location, which matches the coordinates in the letter.

Water is assumed to flow downhill: from the campsites, over the road, through the gully, across the delta, and into the stream. Where a detail could not be confirmed from a photo alone, the description says so. No measurements are claimed beyond those in the letter. Depth and length indications are approximate.

## IMG_7473.jpeg

Landscape view.

Bare sand two-track in the foreground, with tire tracks. A small step in the sand at left-center looks like an early headcut, where the ground drops as water cuts backward into the road. A low sand island with grass splits the flow near the center. The bank at right is bare sand with roots and a fallen branch. The Cut is visible at the end of the road, with a concrete block at the water's edge.

Why it matters: Bare sand carries road runoff and sediment straight into the water. The step and the split around the sand island show flow already cutting across the road surface. Vehicle tracks cross the damaged area, which shows the route drivers are taking around it.

Caption: Gully on the access two-track, looking toward The Cut. Bare sand carries road runoff and sediment into the water, and a headcut has started at the road edge.

## IMG_7474.jpeg

Portrait view.

Uphill side of the access two-track, rising from the foreground toward the campsites. Rutted sand and leaf litter. A tent, a vehicle, and kayaks sit in the campsites at the top of the rise. Grass and leaves cover the shoulder at right.

Why it matters: Shows the uphill route traffic takes past the damage. The campsites at the top are close enough that the road damage could make them unusable.

Caption: Uphill side of the access two-track, rising toward the campsites. Vehicle tracks and bare sand show the route traffic takes around the damage.

## IMG_7475.jpeg

Portrait view.

Downhill side of the access two-track. Exposed sand and roots form a cut bank at left, with branch debris along its edge (as preliminary dam material). The right side is a grassy shoulder. The campsites with a tent and kayaks are visible at the top.

Why it matters: The exposed bank at left is bare sand with no plant cover. Covering and holding that kind of bank is the usual first step in erosion control. It shows erosion reaching the roadside, not only the gully.

Caption: Downhill side of the access two-track. Exposed sand and roots on the left bank show the erosion moving into the roadside. Branch debris along the edge (as preliminary dam material).

## IMG_7476.jpeg

Landscape view.

Closer view of the same spot as IMG_7473. A sand step with an exposed face sits at left, just above the road. A grassy sand mound in the center splits the flow. Bare sand bank and root debris sit at right. The Cut and the concrete block are at the far end.

Why it matters: The clearest view of the headcut at the road edge. It shows where sand has dropped from the road surface toward the water.

Caption: Closer view of the headcut at the road edge. Sand has dropped from the road surface, and water splits around a grassy sand mound before reaching the stream.

## IMG_7478.jpeg

Portrait view.

Dark, damp-looking sand road covered with fallen maple leaves. Grass at left. An RV and a blue car are parked at the top of the frame, at the campsite. A shallow depression at top center appears to run from the campsite down toward the road.

Why it matters: The dark, damp surface and the depression show a flow path from the campsite onto the road. Water runs downhill from the campsites, across the road, and into the gully, carrying sand with it.

Caption: Sandy road surface with runoff channel from the campsite. Dark, damp sand and a shallow depression show the flow path toward the road.
