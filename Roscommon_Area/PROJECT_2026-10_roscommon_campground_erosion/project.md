## Erosion site

- Clean sand gully drains runoff from the two-track directly into The Cut
- Headcuts have advanced into the two-track tread
- Vehicles accelerate erosion and detour further uphill, spreading damage
- Near tipping point: two-track or adjacent camping spots soon unusable

## Proposed approach (pending DNR response)

1. Treat headcuts first: lay back vertical faces to 2:1, cover with brush, butt-ends uphill, staked; hard sill at the lip of the main headcut
2. 3-5 low brush check dams below: 6-12 in tall, keyed 6+ in into bed and banks, center notch lower than ends, filter layer on the upstream face (sand will not seal like silt)
3. Every crest below the two-track edge (prevents flanking into ruts)
4. Armor each landing (brush mat or rock) against plunge-pool scour
5. Space by elevation: base of upper dam at about the crest of the lower dam
6. Controlled arrival: no alternate drainage exists (source checked), so harden a broad dip where the two-track meets the gully and let water enter as a sheet; do not add ditches or turnouts that concentrate more flow here
7. Keep traffic on the tread by design, not signs: make the hardened dip the easy route and block the uphill detour with logs or boulders

## Risks

- Plunge-pool scour undercutting a dam
- Flanking from poor bank attachment
- Surge overtopping that accelerates headcuts instead of retarding them
